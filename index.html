Category performance across all three tests

Pearson prints a bar per content area on each report. Below is every category, every attempt, measured off her actual score reports — plus the 3-test average, which is the most trustworthy column here.

Why the average column matters most: outside of Colorado Law, each content area only holds a handful of scored questions — so getting two extra questions right or wrong can swing a single bar by 20–30 points. That's why these bars jump around so much between attempts. Averaging all three tests cancels most of that noise out and shows what's actually true.
One limitation, stated plainly: Pearson's bars are relative within each report — the longest bar on a report is the reference point, so a category can look like it "dropped" simply because a different category improved more that day. Use the bars for ranking what to study, and use the overall score (40 → 47 → 63) as the measure of real progress.
